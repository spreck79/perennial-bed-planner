Hosta

Hosta hybrids
◆ Garden favorite Deer often eat it! Toxic to dogs and cats

Bold leaves in every shade of green, blue and gold. The backbone of many shade gardens. Deer and slugs love it too.

At a glance

Height12–30 in
Spacing24 in apart (center to center)
Place in the bedMiddle row
BloomsJul
ColorLavender or white spikes; grown for leaves
LightPart shade to shade
Grows in shade, or with up to 6 hours of direct sun a day
Soil moistureAverage to moist
Plant typeClump-forming perennial
Hardiness zones3a to 9b (hardy in Michigan zone 6B)
DeerFrequently severely damaged (Rutgers rating D)
ToxicToxic to dogs and cats
ASPCA lists hosta as toxic

Spring prep
Cut back
  • Spring prep Early Apr: pull mulch off the crown
    Early April, as shoots show: pull back any winter mulch from the crown and clear soggy leaves off the new growth.
  • Cut back Late Oct to Nov, after hard frosts (rake up slug eggs)
    Cut back in fall, after a few hard frosts (usually late October to November): dead hosta leaves hide slug eggs, so rake them all up.
  • Divide Spring, before leaves open
    Divide in spring as the shoots poke up, before the leaves open.

Goes well with

  • Autumn Fern
    Walters Gardens (Zeeland, Michigan) lists varieties of these as companions: Japanese forest grass, Heuchera, Hosta and foamflower. Walters Gardens, Zeeland, Michigan: Autumn Fern companions
  • Bleeding Heart
    Pairs with Virginia bluebells in spring; ferns, hosta, hardy geranium and astilbe fill in as it dies back. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Bleeding Heart article
  • Daffodil
    Purdue Extension: interplant daffodils with late-emerging, sturdy perennials such as hosta, grasses or daylilies. Purdue Extension: Gardening with Bulbs
  • Darwin Hybrid Tulip
    Purdue Extension: plant tulips with later-emerging plants that hide the ripening foliage, especially hosta, daylily and ornamental grasses. Purdue Extension: Gardening with Bulbs
  • Foamflower
    Walters Gardens: foamflower pairs beautifully with hostas and ferns. Walters Gardens, Zeeland, Michigan: Plants to Pair with Hostas
  • Geranium 'Rozanne'
    Walters Gardens (Zeeland, Michigan) lists varieties of these as companions: Heuchera, Hosta and Sedum. Walters Gardens, Zeeland, Michigan: Geranium 'Rozanne' companions
  • Japanese Anemone
    Pair with fall aster, bugbane, eupatorium, goldenrod, sedum or toad lily; near bleeding heart; with variegated hosta and grasses. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Japanese Anemone article
  • Japanese Forest Grass
    Combines well with hosta and ferns; eye-catching near dark-leaved Heuchera, Pulmonaria or Brunnera; a backdrop for dark astilbe. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Japanese Forest Grass article
  • Lenten Rose
    Combines with barrenwort in spring; later a backdrop for hostas; shown among hardy geranium. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Lenten Rose article
  • Maidenhair Fern
    An excellent addition to hosta gardens. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Maidenhair Fern article
  • Masterwort
    Combine with astilbe, ligularia and ferns in shade; fine foliage contrasts with hostas. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Masterwort article
  • Solomon's Seal
    Combines well with bleeding hearts, ferns, lungworts, bergenia, wild ginger, astilbes and hostas. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Solomon's Seal article
  • Toad Lily
    Combines well with anemone, astilbe, ferns, hellebores, hosta and lungwort. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Toad Lily article
  • Virginia Bluebells
    Hostas and ferns both work well as companions; use other plants to hide gaps after it goes dormant. Perennials.com: Virginia Bluebells profile, Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Virginia Bluebells article
  • Wild Blue Phlox
    Goes well with Jack-in-the-pulpit; combine with hosta, astilbe, bleeding heart, columbine, hellebore, heuchera, lungwort and ferns. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Wild Blue Phlox article
  • Wild Columbine
    Combines well with hostas and astilbe in shade gardens, and with wild geranium and wild ginger in natural settings. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Wild Columbine article
  • Yellow Foxglove
    Combines with catmint and lady's mantle; in shade with astilbe, hosta or coral bells. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Yellow Foxglove article
