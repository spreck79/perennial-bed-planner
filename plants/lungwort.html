Lungwort

Pulmonaria saccharata
◆ Garden favorite

One of the first flowers of spring, pink and blue on the same plant, over silver-spotted leaves that brighten shade all season.

At a glance

Height9–12 in
Spacing15 in apart (center to center)
Place in the bedFront row
BloomsApr – May
ColorPink buds open to blue
LightPart shade to shade
Grows in shade, or with up to 6 hours of direct sun a day
Soil moistureAverage to moist
Plant typeLow, spreading groundcover
Hardiness zonesNot recorded yet
DeerRarely damaged (Rutgers rating A)
ToxicBelieved non-toxic to people, dogs and cats

Bloom
Cut back
  • Cut back Early Apr: snip winter-browned leaves
    Evergreen or semi-evergreen: no fall cutting. In early spring, snip off leaves that winter browned.
  • Divide Late Aug to Sep
    Divide in late August to September, if the clump is crowded or flowers less. Allow 4 to 6 weeks before the ground freezes.

Goes well with

  • Japanese Forest Grass
    Combines well with hosta and ferns; eye-catching near dark-leaved Heuchera, Pulmonaria or Brunnera; a backdrop for dark astilbe. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Japanese Forest Grass article
  • Solomon's Seal
    Combines well with bleeding hearts, ferns, lungworts, bergenia, wild ginger, astilbes and hostas. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Solomon's Seal article
  • Toad Lily
    Combines well with anemone, astilbe, ferns, hellebores, hosta and lungwort. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Toad Lily article
  • Wild Blue Phlox
    Goes well with Jack-in-the-pulpit; combine with hosta, astilbe, bleeding heart, columbine, hellebore, heuchera, lungwort and ferns. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Wild Blue Phlox article
