Siberian Iris

Iris sibirica
◆ Garden favorite! Toxic to people, dogs and cats

Elegant blue flowers in early summer over grassy leaves that look good all season. Tougher than bearded iris and fine in damp soil.

Spring prep
Cut back
  • Spring prep Early Apr: pull mulch off the crown
    Early April, as shoots show: pull back any winter mulch from the crown and clear soggy leaves off the new growth.
  • Cut back Mid-Apr to mid-May: cut to 8 to 24 in
    Spring cleanup, once days stay around 50°F (mid-April to mid-May): cut last year's stems down to 8 to 24 in. New growth hides the stubble within weeks, and native bees nest in the hollow stems.
  • Divide Late Aug to Sep, if crowded
    Divide only if crowded, in late August to September (fleshy roots do best in fall). Replant peony eyes just 1½ in deep.

  • Lady's Mantle
    Walters Gardens (Zeeland, Michigan) lists varieties of these as companions: lady's mantle, astilbe, Baptisia and Salvia 'May Night'. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Lady's Mantle article, Walters Gardens, Zeeland, Michigan: Siberian Iris companions
  • Cardinal Flower
    Combines well with great blue lobelia, irises and marsh milkweed; also found with purple coneflower, blazing star and foxglove beardtongue. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Cardinal Flower article
  • Garden Peony
    NDSU Extension: plants that pair well with peonies include irises, catmint, coral bells and June-blooming alliums. NDSU Extension (North Dakota State University): Peonies
  • Goat's Beard
    Combines well with astilbe, columbine and ferns; plant with wild ginger, Virginia bluebells, Jacob's ladder and wild geranium; mix with iris. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Goat's Beard article
