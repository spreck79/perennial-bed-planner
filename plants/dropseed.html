Prairie Dropseed

Sporobolus heterolepis
Michigan native

A fine-textured fountain of grass with airy late-summer flowers that smell faintly of popcorn. Slow to start, long-lived.

At a glance

Height15–30 in
Spacing24 in apart (center to center)
Place in the bedMiddle row
BloomsAug – Sep
ColorAiry, fragrant pink-tan sprays
LightFull sun
Needs 6 or more hours of direct sun a day
Soil moistureDry to average
Plant typeGrass or sedge
Hardiness zones3a to 9b (hardy in Michigan zone 6B)
DeerNot rated by Rutgers
ToxicBelieved non-toxic to people, dogs and cats

Bloom
Cut back
  • Cut back Early Apr: cut to 3 to 4 in
    Leave standing all winter; it looks good in snow. Cut to 3 to 4 in tall in early April, before the new green blades come up.
  • Divide Spring, as shoots come up
    Divide in spring as new growth starts, if the clump gets too big.

Goes well with

  • Black-eyed SusanLanceleaf CoreopsisLittle BluestemPurple ConeflowerSedum 'Autumn Joy'
    Combined with fall-blooming sedums; a great filler between purple coneflower, liatris, black-eyed Susan and butterfly weed. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Prairie Dropseed article
  • Blazing Star
    Combined with fall-blooming sedums; a great filler between purple coneflower, liatris, black-eyed Susan and butterfly weed. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Blazing Star article, Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Prairie Dropseed article
  • Butterfly Weed
    Combined with fall-blooming sedums; a great filler between purple coneflower, liatris, black-eyed Susan and butterfly weed. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Butterfly Weed article, Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Prairie Dropseed article
  • Allium 'Purple Sensation'
    Purdue Extension: alliums look good planted with grasses, in cottage plantings or English borders. Purdue Extension: Gardening with Bulbs
  • Anise Hyssop
    Combines well with purple coneflower, bee balm and native grasses; in gardens with Japanese anemones. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Anise Hyssop article
  • Daffodil
    Purdue Extension: interplant daffodils with late-emerging, sturdy perennials such as hosta, grasses or daylilies. Purdue Extension: Gardening with Bulbs
  • Darwin Hybrid Tulip
    Purdue Extension: plant tulips with later-emerging plants that hide the ripening foliage, especially hosta, daylily and ornamental grasses. Purdue Extension: Gardening with Bulbs
  • Foxglove Beardtongue
    Combines well with blue spiderworts, blue false indigo and prairie dropseed; try it with catmint and lady's mantle. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Foxglove Beardtongue article
  • Japanese Anemone
    Pair with fall aster, bugbane, eupatorium, goldenrod, sedum or toad lily; near bleeding heart; with variegated hosta and grasses. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Japanese Anemone article
  • Prairie Smoke
    Grows well with blazing star and little bluestem; often grows with prairie dropseed in natural settings. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Prairie Smoke article
  • Sneezeweed
    Combines well with ornamental grasses, anise hyssop, beebalm, daylilies, fall asters, liatris, purple coneflower and rudbeckias. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Sneezeweed article
