Brunnera 'Jack Frost'

Brunnera macrophylla
◆ Garden favorite

Clouds of forget-me-not flowers in spring, then silvery heart-shaped leaves that light up the shade all season.

At a glance

Height12–18 in
Spacing18 in apart (center to center)
Place in the bedMiddle row
BloomsApr – May
ColorTiny blue flowers, silver leaves
LightPart shade to shade; moist soil
Grows in shade, or with up to 6 hours of direct sun a day
Soil moistureAverage to moist
Plant typeClump-forming perennial
Hardiness zones3a to 8b (hardy in Michigan zone 6B)
DeerRarely damaged (Rutgers rating A)
ToxicBelieved non-toxic to people, dogs and cats

Spring prep
Cut back
  • Spring prep Early Apr: pull mulch off the crown
    Early April, as shoots show: pull back any winter mulch from the crown and clear soggy leaves off the new growth.
  • Cut back Late Oct to Nov, after hard frosts (mushy leaves)
    Cut back in fall, after a few hard frosts (usually late October to November): the leaves turn to mush and add nothing to the winter garden.
  • Divide Late Aug to Sep
    Divide in late August to September, if the clump is crowded or flowers less. Allow 4 to 6 weeks before the ground freezes.

Goes well with

  • AstilbeBleeding HeartCoral BellsJapanese Painted Fern
    Contrasts nicely with Japanese painted fern; combines well with astilbes, bleeding heart and Heuchera. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Brunnera 'Jack Frost' article
  • Japanese Forest Grass
    Combines well with hosta and ferns; eye-catching near dark-leaved Heuchera, Pulmonaria or Brunnera; a backdrop for dark astilbe. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Japanese Forest Grass article
  • Morrow's Sedge
    Walters Gardens (Zeeland, Michigan) lists varieties of these as companions: astilbe, lady fern 'Lady in Red', Brunnera 'Jack Frost' and Heuchera. Walters Gardens, Zeeland, Michigan: Morrow's Sedge companions
