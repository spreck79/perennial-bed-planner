Coral Bells

Heuchera, garden hybrids
◆ Garden favorite

Grown mostly for its colorful leaves, which look good spring through fall. The ASPCA lists coral bells as non-toxic to dogs and cats.

At a glance

Height10–18 in
Spacing13 in apart (center to center)
Place in the bedFront row
BloomsMay – Jul
ColorPink wands; purple or lime leaves
LightPart shade; well-drained soil, not soggy
Needs 3 to 6 hours of direct sun a day
Soil moistureAverage
Plant typeLow, spreading groundcover
Hardiness zones4a to 9b (hardy in Michigan zone 6B)
DeerSeldom severely damaged (Rutgers rating B)
ToxicBelieved non-toxic to people, dogs and cats

  • Cut back Early Apr: snip winter-browned leaves
    Evergreen or semi-evergreen: no fall cutting. In early spring, snip off leaves that winter browned.
  • Optional trim Optional: shear by half in midsummer
    Optional: if it gets floppy or flowering slows in midsummer, shear it back by about half for fresh growth and more flowers.
  • Divide Late Aug to Sep
    Divide in late August to September, if the clump is crowded or flowers less. Allow 4 to 6 weeks before the ground freezes.

Goes well with

  • Autumn Fern
    Walters Gardens (Zeeland, Michigan) lists varieties of these as companions: Japanese forest grass, Heuchera, Hosta and foamflower. Walters Gardens, Zeeland, Michigan: Autumn Fern companions
  • Brunnera 'Jack Frost'
    Contrasts nicely with Japanese painted fern; combines well with astilbes, bleeding heart and Heuchera. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Brunnera 'Jack Frost' article
  • Garden Peony
    NDSU Extension: plants that pair well with peonies include irises, catmint, coral bells and June-blooming alliums. NDSU Extension (North Dakota State University): Peonies
  • Geranium 'Rozanne'
    Walters Gardens (Zeeland, Michigan) lists varieties of these as companions: Heuchera, Hosta and Sedum. Walters Gardens, Zeeland, Michigan: Geranium 'Rozanne' companions
  • Japanese Forest Grass
    Combines well with hosta and ferns; eye-catching near dark-leaved Heuchera, Pulmonaria or Brunnera; a backdrop for dark astilbe. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Japanese Forest Grass article
  • Little Bluestem
    Pair with dark-leaved Heuchera; combine with aster, tall sedums like 'Autumn Joy', yarrow, rudbeckias and purple coneflower. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Little Bluestem article
  • Morrow's Sedge
    Walters Gardens (Zeeland, Michigan) lists varieties of these as companions: astilbe, lady fern 'Lady in Red', Brunnera 'Jack Frost' and Heuchera. Walters Gardens, Zeeland, Michigan: Morrow's Sedge companions
  • Pennsylvania Sedge
    Heuchera 'Brownies' gives a bolder contrast to the sedge's fine leaves at a woodland edge. Mt. Cuba Center: Pennsylvania Sedge
  • Wild Blue Phlox
    Goes well with Jack-in-the-pulpit; combine with hosta, astilbe, bleeding heart, columbine, hellebore, heuchera, lungwort and ferns. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Wild Blue Phlox article
  • Yellow Foxglove
    Combines with catmint and lady's mantle; in shade with astilbe, hosta or coral bells. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Yellow Foxglove article
