Lamb's Ear

Stachys byzantina
◆ Garden favorite

Velvety silver leaves that kids love to touch, making a bright mat at the front of a sunny bed. Cut off the flower stalks to keep it neat.

At a glance

Height12–18 in
Spacing18 in apart (center to center)
Place in the bedMiddle row
BloomsGrown for its leaves
ColorSoft, fuzzy silver leaves
LightFull sun
Needs 6 or more hours of direct sun a day
Soil moistureDry to average
Plant typeLow, spreading groundcover
Hardiness zones4a to 8b (hardy in Michigan zone 6B)
DeerRarely damaged (Rutgers rating A)
ToxicBelieved non-toxic to people, dogs and cats
Code on the planLaEa

Through the year

AprMayJunJulAugSepOctNov
Cut back
  • Cut back Early Apr: snip winter-browned leaves
    Evergreen or semi-evergreen: no fall cutting. In early spring, snip off leaves that winter browned.
  • Divide Spring, as shoots come up
    Divide in spring as the shoots come up, if the clump is crowded or the middle dies out.

Goes well with

  • Blazing Star
    Combines with yellow flowers such as coreopsis and goldenrod, pink ones such as purple coneflower, prairie grasses and silver plants such as Stachys (lamb's ear). Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Blazing Star article
