Lady Fern

Athyrium filix-femina
Michigan native

A soft, lacy fern that fills the middle of the bed. It looks best with steady moisture and is tidier than most large ferns.

At a glance

Height24–36 in
Spacing24 in apart (center to center)
Place in the bedBack row
BloomsGrown for its leaves
ColorLacy, light green fronds
LightPart shade to shade; moist soil
Grows in shade, or with up to 6 hours of direct sun a day
Soil moistureMoist
Plant typeFern
Hardiness zones4a to 8b (hardy in Michigan zone 6B)
DeerNot rated by Rutgers
ToxicBelieved non-toxic to people, dogs and cats
Code on the planLaFe

Cut back
  • Cut back Early Apr: clear old fronds
    Leave the old fronds over winter to protect the crown. In early April, clear them away before the new fiddleheads uncurl.
  • Divide Spring, as shoots come up
    Divide in spring as new growth starts, if the clump gets too big.

Goes well with

  • Bleeding Heart
    Pairs with Virginia bluebells in spring; ferns, hosta, hardy geranium and astilbe fill in as it dies back. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Bleeding Heart article
  • Foamflower
    Walters Gardens: foamflower pairs beautifully with hostas and ferns. Walters Gardens, Zeeland, Michigan: Plants to Pair with Hostas
  • Goat's Beard
    Combines well with astilbe, columbine and ferns; plant with wild ginger, Virginia bluebells, Jacob's ladder and wild geranium; mix with iris. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Goat's Beard article
  • Japanese Forest Grass
    Combines well with hosta and ferns; eye-catching near dark-leaved Heuchera, Pulmonaria or Brunnera; a backdrop for dark astilbe. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Japanese Forest Grass article
  • Lady's Mantle
    Contrasts nicely with vertical perennials such as iris, liatris, ferns and salvia. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Lady's Mantle article
  • Masterwort
    Combine with astilbe, ligularia and ferns in shade; fine foliage contrasts with hostas. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Masterwort article
  • Morrow's Sedge
    Walters Gardens (Zeeland, Michigan) lists varieties of these as companions: astilbe, lady fern 'Lady in Red', Brunnera 'Jack Frost' and Heuchera. Walters Gardens, Zeeland, Michigan: Morrow's Sedge companions
  • Solomon's Seal
    Combines well with bleeding hearts, ferns, lungworts, bergenia, wild ginger, astilbes and hostas. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Solomon's Seal article
  • Toad Lily
    Combines well with anemone, astilbe, ferns, hellebores, hosta and lungwort. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Toad Lily article
  • Virginia Bluebells
    Hostas and ferns both work well as companions; use other plants to hide gaps after it goes dormant. Perennials.com: Virginia Bluebells profile, Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Virginia Bluebells article
  • Wild Blue Phlox
    Goes well with Jack-in-the-pulpit; combine with hosta, astilbe, bleeding heart, columbine, hellebore, heuchera, lungwort and ferns. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Wild Blue Phlox article
  • Wild Geranium
    Found with Solomon's seal, ferns, mayapple and woodland phlox; combines with celandine poppy, columbine, foamflower and goat's beard. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Wild Geranium article
