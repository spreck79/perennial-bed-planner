Solomon's Seal

Polygonatum biflorum
Michigan native Deer sometimes eat it! Toxic to people, dogs and cats

Graceful arching stems hung with creamy bells in late spring, then blue-black berries. Tough in dry shade and spreads slowly into a colony.

Spring prep
Cut back
  • Spring prep Early Apr: pull mulch off the crown
    Early April, as shoots show: pull back any winter mulch from the crown and clear soggy leaves off the new growth.
  • Cut back Mid-Apr to mid-May: cut to 8 to 24 in
    Spring cleanup, once days stay around 50°F (mid-April to mid-May): cut last year's stems down to 8 to 24 in. New growth hides the stubble within weeks, and native bees nest in the hollow stems.
  • Divide Late Aug to Sep
    Divide in late August to September, if the clump is crowded or flowers less. Allow 4 to 6 weeks before the ground freezes.

Goes well with

  • AstilbeBergeniaBleeding HeartChristmas FernCinnamon FernHostaLady FernLungwortMaidenhair FernOstrich FernWild Ginger
    Combines well with bleeding hearts, ferns, lungworts, bergenia, wild ginger, astilbes and hostas. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Solomon's Seal article
  • Virginia Bluebells
    Combines with bleeding heart and celandine poppy; hosta, astilbe, bugbane, Solomon's seal or ferns fill the gap after it goes dormant. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Virginia Bluebells article
  • Wild Geranium
    Found with Solomon's seal, ferns, mayapple and woodland phlox; combines with celandine poppy, columbine, foamflower and goat's beard. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Wild Geranium article
