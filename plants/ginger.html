Wild Ginger

Asarum canadense
Michigan native! Toxic to people, dogs and cats

A dense groundcover of soft, heart-shaped leaves for shade. The little maroon spring flowers hide at ground level under the leaves.

At a glance

Height4–8 in
Spacing12 in apart (center to center)
Place in the bedFront row
BloomsGrown for its leaves
ColorVelvety heart-shaped leaves
LightPart shade to shade
Grows in shade, or with up to 6 hours of direct sun a day
Soil moistureAverage to moist
Plant typeLow, spreading groundcover; good for filling gaps
Hardiness zones4a to 6b (hardy in Michigan zone 6B)
DeerRarely damaged (Rutgers rating A)
ToxicToxic to people, dogs and cats
Not culinary ginger; contains aristolochic acid (general references; not on the ASPCA list)
Code on the planWiGi

Through the year

AprMayJunJulAugSepOctNov
Cut back
  • Cut back Early Apr: snip winter-browned leaves
    Evergreen or semi-evergreen: no fall cutting. In early spring, snip off leaves that winter browned.
  • Divide Spring, as shoots come up
    Divide in spring as the shoots come up, if the clump is crowded or the middle dies out.

Goes well with

  • Goat's Beard
    Combines well with astilbe, columbine and ferns; plant with wild ginger, Virginia bluebells, Jacob's ladder and wild geranium; mix with iris. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Goat's Beard article
  • Solomon's Seal
    Combines well with bleeding hearts, ferns, lungworts, bergenia, wild ginger, astilbes and hostas. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Solomon's Seal article
  • Virginia Waterleaf
    Commonly found with wild columbine, wild ginger, wild geranium, woodland phlox and mayapple. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Virginia Waterleaf article
  • Wild Columbine
    Combines well with hostas and astilbe in shade gardens, and with wild geranium and wild ginger in natural settings. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Wild Columbine article
