Wild Columbine

Aquilegia canadensis
Michigan native

Dangling red-and-yellow flowers in spring that hummingbirds love. Short-lived but reseeds gently.

  • Spring prep Early Apr: pull mulch off the crown
    Early April, as shoots show: pull back any winter mulch from the crown and clear soggy leaves off the new growth.
  • Cut back Mid-Apr to mid-May: cut to 8 to 24 in
    Spring cleanup, once days stay around 50°F (mid-April to mid-May): cut last year's stems down to 8 to 24 in. New growth hides the stubble within weeks, and native bees nest in the hollow stems.
  • Optional trim Optional: shear after first flowers
    Optional: once the first flowers fade, shear the old stems back to the new leaves at the base. Many rebloom in 5 to 6 weeks.
  • Divide Late Aug to Sep
    Divide in late August to September, if the clump is crowded or flowers less. Allow 4 to 6 weeks before the ground freezes.

  • Bottlebrush Grass
    Minnesota Board of Water and Soil Resources: good companion plants include early meadow rue, wild columbine and zigzag goldenrod. Minnesota Board of Water and Soil Resources: featured plant, Bottlebrush Grass
  • Goat's Beard
    Combines well with astilbe, columbine and ferns; plant with wild ginger, Virginia bluebells, Jacob's ladder and wild geranium; mix with iris. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Goat's Beard article
  • Virginia Waterleaf
    Commonly found with wild columbine, wild ginger, wild geranium, woodland phlox and mayapple. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Virginia Waterleaf article
  • Wild Blue Phlox
    Goes well with Jack-in-the-pulpit; combine with hosta, astilbe, bleeding heart, columbine, hellebore, heuchera, lungwort and ferns. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Wild Blue Phlox article
  • Woodland Sunflower
    New Moon Nurseries: in lightly shaded gardens, companions include wild columbine, Pennsylvania sedge and lanceleaf coreopsis. New Moon Nursery: Woodland Sunflower
