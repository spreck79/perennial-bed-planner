Japanese Forest Grass

Hakonechloa macra 'Aureola'
◆ Garden favorite

Gold blades spill over the bed edge like a waterfall and turn coppery in fall. Slow to get going, but long-lived.

At a glance

Height12–18 in
Spacing18 in apart (center to center)
Place in the bedMiddle row
BloomsGrown for its leaves
ColorGolden, cascading blades
LightPart shade; moist, rich soil
Needs 3 to 6 hours of direct sun a day
Soil moistureAverage to moist
Plant typeGrass or sedge
Hardiness zones5a to 9b (hardy in Michigan zone 6B)
DeerRarely damaged (Rutgers rating A)
ToxicBelieved non-toxic to people, dogs and cats
Code on the planJFGr

Through the year

AprMayJunJulAugSepOctNov
Cut back
  • Cut back Early Apr: cut to 3 to 4 in
    Leave standing all winter; it looks good in snow. Cut to 3 to 4 in tall in early April, before the new green blades come up.
  • Divide Spring, as shoots come up
    Divide in spring as new growth starts, if the clump gets too big.

    Walters Gardens (Zeeland, Michigan) lists varieties of these as companions: Japanese forest grass, Heuchera, Hosta and foamflower. Walters Gardens, Zeeland, Michigan: Autumn Fern companions
