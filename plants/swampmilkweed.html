Swamp Milkweed

Asclepias incarnata
Michigan native! Toxic to people, dogs and cats

A clump-forming milkweed for moist spots, with fragrant pink flowers. A monarch host plant that does not run like common milkweed.

Spring prep
Cut back
  • Spring prep Early Apr: pull mulch off the crown
    Early April, as shoots show: pull back any winter mulch from the crown and clear soggy leaves off the new growth.
  • Cut back Mid-Apr to mid-May: cut to 8 to 24 in
    Spring cleanup, once days stay around 50°F (mid-April to mid-May): cut last year's stems down to 8 to 24 in. New growth hides the stubble within weeks, and native bees nest in the hollow stems.
  • Divide Spring, as shoots come up
    Divide in spring as the shoots come up, if the clump is crowded or the middle dies out.

Goes well with

  • Cardinal Flower
    Combines well with great blue lobelia, irises and marsh milkweed; also found with purple coneflower, blazing star and foxglove beardtongue. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Cardinal Flower article
  • Mountain Mint
    Minnesota Board of Water and Soil Resources: pairs well with other moist prairie plants, including rattlesnake master and swamp milkweed. Minnesota Board of Water and Soil Resources: featured plant, Mountain Mint
  • Palm Sedge
    New Moon Nurseries: try pairing with swamp milkweed, turtlehead, Joe-Pye weed (Eupatorium purpureum) or great blue lobelia. New Moon Nursery: Palm Sedge
