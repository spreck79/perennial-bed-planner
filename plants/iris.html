Bearded Iris

Iris germanica
◆ Garden favorite! Toxic to people, dogs and cats

Ruffled flowers in late spring over sword-like leaves. Plant the rhizomes at the soil surface; divide every 3-4 years.

  • Spring prep Early Apr: pull mulch off the crown
    Early April, as shoots show: pull back any winter mulch from the crown and clear soggy leaves off the new growth.
  • Cut back Late Oct to Nov, after hard frosts (iris borer eggs)
    Cut back in fall, after a few hard frosts (usually late October to November): old leaves are where iris borers lay their eggs.
  • Divide Late Jul to early Aug
    Divide every 3 to 4 years in late July to early August.

Goes well with

  • Creeping Phlox
    Walters Gardens (Zeeland, Michigan) lists varieties of these as companions: Bergenia and bearded iris. Walters Gardens, Zeeland, Michigan: Creeping Phlox companions
  • Garden Peony
    NDSU Extension: plants that pair well with peonies include irises, catmint, coral bells and June-blooming alliums. NDSU Extension (North Dakota State University): Peonies
  • Lady's Mantle
    Contrasts nicely with vertical perennials such as iris, liatris, ferns and salvia. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Lady's Mantle article
  • Shasta Daisy
    Walters Gardens (Zeeland, Michigan) lists varieties of these as companions: yarrow, daylily, iris, catmint and sedum. Walters Gardens, Zeeland, Michigan: Shasta Daisy companions
