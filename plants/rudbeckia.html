Black-eyed Susan

Rudbeckia fulgida
Michigan native✿ Daisy family

A long run of golden daisies from midsummer into fall, loved by bees and butterflies. Leave the seed heads for winter birds. Daisy family, like ragweed.

Spring prep
Cut back
  • Spring prep Early Apr: pull mulch off the crown
    Early April, as shoots show: pull back any winter mulch from the crown and clear soggy leaves off the new growth.
  • Cut back Mid-Apr to mid-May: cut to 8 to 24 in
    Spring cleanup, once days stay around 50°F (mid-April to mid-May): cut last year's stems down to 8 to 24 in. New growth hides the stubble within weeks, and native bees nest in the hollow stems.
  • Divide Spring, as shoots come up
    Divide in spring as the shoots come up, if the clump is crowded or the middle dies out.

Goes well with

  • Garden PhloxSedum 'Autumn Joy'
    Walters Gardens (Zeeland, Michigan) lists varieties of these as companions: Sedum 'Autumn Joy' and garden phlox. Walters Gardens, Zeeland, Michigan: Black-eyed Susan companions
  • Butterfly Weed
    Combine with Penstemon 'Husker Red' or purple speedwells; works well with coreopsis, purple coneflower, blazing star, black-eyed Susan, little bluestem and prairie dropseed. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Butterfly Weed article
  • Little Bluestem
    Pair with dark-leaved Heuchera; combine with aster, tall sedums like 'Autumn Joy', yarrow, rudbeckias and purple coneflower. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Little Bluestem article
  • Prairie Dropseed
    Combined with fall-blooming sedums; a great filler between purple coneflower, liatris, black-eyed Susan and butterfly weed. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Prairie Dropseed article
  • Sneezeweed
    Combines well with ornamental grasses, anise hyssop, beebalm, daylilies, fall asters, liatris, purple coneflower and rudbeckias. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Sneezeweed article
