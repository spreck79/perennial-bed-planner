Prairie Alumroot

Heuchera richardsonii
Michigan native

The Michigan native cousin of coral bells: a low mound of scalloped leaves with tall, airy flower wands in late spring that bees and hummingbirds visit. Tough in dry soil.

At a glance

Height18–30 in
Spacing15 in apart (center to center)
Place in the bedMiddle row
BloomsMay – Jul
ColorGreen-cream flower wands over scalloped leaves
LightFull sun to part shade
Needs at least 3 hours of direct sun a day
Soil moistureDry to average
Plant typeClump-forming perennial
Hardiness zones3a to 9b (hardy in Michigan zone 6B)
DeerSeldom severely damaged (Rutgers rating B)
ToxicBelieved non-toxic to people, dogs and cats

  • Cut back Early Apr: snip winter-browned leaves
    Evergreen or semi-evergreen: no fall cutting. In early spring, snip off leaves that winter browned.
  • Optional trim Optional: shear after first flowers
    Optional: once the first flowers fade, shear the old stems back to the new leaves at the base. Many rebloom in 5 to 6 weeks.
  • Divide Late Aug to Sep
    Divide in late August to September, if the clump is crowded or flowers less. Allow 4 to 6 weeks before the ground freezes.

Goes well with

  • Little Bluestem
    Pair with dark-leaved Heuchera; combine with aster, tall sedums like 'Autumn Joy', yarrow, rudbeckias and purple coneflower. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Little Bluestem article
  • Wild Blue Phlox
    Goes well with Jack-in-the-pulpit; combine with hosta, astilbe, bleeding heart, columbine, hellebore, heuchera, lungwort and ferns. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Wild Blue Phlox article
