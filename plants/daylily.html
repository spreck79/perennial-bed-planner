Daylily

Hemerocallis hybrids
◆ Garden favorite Deer sometimes eat it! Toxic to cats

Tough, adaptable clumps of strappy leaves with a parade of lily-like flowers. Each flower lasts one day.

At a glance

Height18–30 in
Spacing18 in apart (center to center)
Place in the bedMiddle row
BloomsJun – Jul
ColorGold, orange, red or pink
LightFull sun to part shade
Needs at least 3 hours of direct sun a day
Soil moistureAdaptable
Plant typeClump-forming perennial
Hardiness zones3a to 9b (hardy in Michigan zone 6B)
DeerOccasionally severely damaged (Rutgers rating C)
ToxicToxic to cats
ASPCA: toxic to cats (can cause kidney failure)

Spring prep
Cut back
  • Spring prep Early Apr: pull mulch off the crown
    Early April, as shoots show: pull back any winter mulch from the crown and clear soggy leaves off the new growth.
  • Cut back Late Oct to Nov, after hard frosts (mushy leaves)
    Cut back in fall, after a few hard frosts (usually late October to November): the leaves turn to mush and add nothing to the winter garden.
  • Divide Spring, as shoots come up
    Divide in spring as the shoots come up, if the clump is crowded or the middle dies out.

Goes well with

  • Catmint 'Walker's Low'
    Combines well with beebalm and Shasta daisies; harmonizes with yellow foxglove, Coreopsis and lemon daylilies. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Catmint 'Walker's Low' article
  • Daffodil
    Purdue Extension: interplant daffodils with late-emerging, sturdy perennials such as hosta, grasses or daylilies. Purdue Extension: Gardening with Bulbs
  • Darwin Hybrid Tulip
    Purdue Extension: plant tulips with later-emerging plants that hide the ripening foliage, especially hosta, daylily and ornamental grasses. Purdue Extension: Gardening with Bulbs
  • Shasta Daisy
    Walters Gardens (Zeeland, Michigan) lists varieties of these as companions: yarrow, daylily, iris, catmint and sedum. Walters Gardens, Zeeland, Michigan: Shasta Daisy companions
  • Sneezeweed
    Combines well with ornamental grasses, anise hyssop, beebalm, daylilies, fall asters, liatris, purple coneflower and rudbeckias. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Sneezeweed article
