Astilbe

Astilbe, garden hybrids
◆ Garden favorite

The classic shade bloomer, with feathery plumes in early summer that dry and stand into winter. Water in dry spells or the leaves crisp.

At a glance

Height18–30 in
Spacing18 in apart (center to center)
Place in the bedMiddle row
BloomsJun – Jul
ColorPink, red or white plumes
LightPart shade; soil must stay moist
Needs 3 to 6 hours of direct sun a day
Soil moistureMoist
Plant typeClump-forming perennial
Hardiness zones4a to 8b (hardy in Michigan zone 6B)
DeerSeldom severely damaged (Rutgers rating B)
ToxicBelieved non-toxic to people, dogs and cats

Spring prep
Cut back
  • Spring prep Early Apr: pull mulch off the crown
    Early April, as shoots show: pull back any winter mulch from the crown and clear soggy leaves off the new growth.
  • Cut back Mid-Apr to mid-May: cut to 8 to 24 in
    Spring cleanup, once days stay around 50°F (mid-April to mid-May): cut last year's stems down to 8 to 24 in. New growth hides the stubble within weeks, and native bees nest in the hollow stems.
  • Divide Spring, as shoots come up
    Divide in spring as the shoots come up, if the clump is crowded or the middle dies out.

Goes well with

  • Bleeding Heart
    Pairs with Virginia bluebells in spring; ferns, hosta, hardy geranium and astilbe fill in as it dies back. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Bleeding Heart article
  • Brunnera 'Jack Frost'
    Contrasts nicely with Japanese painted fern; combines well with astilbes, bleeding heart and Heuchera. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Brunnera 'Jack Frost' article
  • Goat's Beard
    Combines well with astilbe, columbine and ferns; plant with wild ginger, Virginia bluebells, Jacob's ladder and wild geranium; mix with iris. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Goat's Beard article
  • Japanese Forest Grass
    Combines well with hosta and ferns; eye-catching near dark-leaved Heuchera, Pulmonaria or Brunnera; a backdrop for dark astilbe. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Japanese Forest Grass article
  • Masterwort
    Combine with astilbe, ligularia and ferns in shade; fine foliage contrasts with hostas. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Masterwort article
  • Morrow's Sedge
    Walters Gardens (Zeeland, Michigan) lists varieties of these as companions: astilbe, lady fern 'Lady in Red', Brunnera 'Jack Frost' and Heuchera. Walters Gardens, Zeeland, Michigan: Morrow's Sedge companions
  • Siberian Iris
    Walters Gardens (Zeeland, Michigan) lists varieties of these as companions: lady's mantle, astilbe, Baptisia and Salvia 'May Night'. Walters Gardens, Zeeland, Michigan: Siberian Iris companions
  • Solomon's Seal
    Combines well with bleeding hearts, ferns, lungworts, bergenia, wild ginger, astilbes and hostas. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Solomon's Seal article
  • Toad Lily
    Combines well with anemone, astilbe, ferns, hellebores, hosta and lungwort. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Toad Lily article
  • Virginia Bluebells
    Combines with bleeding heart and celandine poppy; hosta, astilbe, bugbane, Solomon's seal or ferns fill the gap after it goes dormant. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Virginia Bluebells article
  • Wild Blue Phlox
    Goes well with Jack-in-the-pulpit; combine with hosta, astilbe, bleeding heart, columbine, hellebore, heuchera, lungwort and ferns. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Wild Blue Phlox article
  • Wild Columbine
    Combines well with hostas and astilbe in shade gardens, and with wild geranium and wild ginger in natural settings. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Wild Columbine article
  • Yellow Foxglove
    Combines with catmint and lady's mantle; in shade with astilbe, hosta or coral bells. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Yellow Foxglove article
