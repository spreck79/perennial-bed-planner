Lanceleaf Coreopsis

Coreopsis lanceolata
Michigan native✿ Daisy family

Cheerful yellow flowers from late spring into summer; cut back spent stems for more blooms. Thrives in poor, dry soil. Daisy family, like ragweed.

  • Spring prep Early Apr: pull mulch off the crown
    Early April, as shoots show: pull back any winter mulch from the crown and clear soggy leaves off the new growth.
  • Cut back Mid-Apr to mid-May: cut to 8 to 24 in
    Spring cleanup, once days stay around 50°F (mid-April to mid-May): cut last year's stems down to 8 to 24 in. New growth hides the stubble within weeks, and native bees nest in the hollow stems.
  • Optional trim Optional: shear after first flowers
    Optional: once the first flowers fade, shear the old stems back to the new leaves at the base. Many rebloom in 5 to 6 weeks.
  • Divide Late Aug to Sep
    Divide in late August to September, if the clump is crowded or flowers less. Allow 4 to 6 weeks before the ground freezes.

Goes well with

  • Blazing Star
    Combines with yellow flowers such as coreopsis and goldenrod, pink ones such as purple coneflower, prairie grasses and silver plants such as Stachys (lamb's ear). Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Blazing Star article
  • Butterfly Weed
    Combine with Penstemon 'Husker Red' or purple speedwells; works well with coreopsis, purple coneflower, blazing star, black-eyed Susan, little bluestem and prairie dropseed. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Butterfly Weed article
  • Catmint 'Walker's Low'
    Combines well with beebalm and Shasta daisies; harmonizes with yellow foxglove, Coreopsis and lemon daylilies. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Catmint 'Walker's Low' article
  • Prairie Dropseed
    Combined with fall-blooming sedums; a great filler between purple coneflower, liatris, black-eyed Susan and butterfly weed. Wisconsin Horticulture (UW–Madison Extension): Prairie Dropseed article
  • Sea Holly
    Walters Gardens (Zeeland, Michigan) lists varieties of these as companions: Liatris and Coreopsis. Walters Gardens, Zeeland, Michigan: Sea Holly companions
  • Wild Strawberry
    Izel Native Plants: plant wild strawberry intermingled with lanceleaf coreopsis and purple coneflower. Izel Native Plants: Wild Strawberry
  • Woodland Sunflower
    New Moon Nurseries: in lightly shaded gardens, companions include wild columbine, Pennsylvania sedge and lanceleaf coreopsis. New Moon Nursery: Woodland Sunflower
