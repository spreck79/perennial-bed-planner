Quick start

Work down the steps on the left of the planner. Want to see it walked through? Press Guided tour at the top of the planner. Just looking around? Load an example garden in step 1.

  1. Name your gardenOptional. It goes at the top of the planner and on your PDF guide.
  2. What kind of garden?Michigan natives, garden favorites or both, and the kinds of plants you like.
  3. Plants to watch out forLeave out plants that are toxic to pets or people, that deer eat, that need winter care, or that bother ragweed allergies.
  4. Build your bedA rectangle, a round island, or draw your own. Set the size with − and +.
  5. Add objectsPaths, stepping stones, rocks, a birdbath, shrubs or trees. Plants are laid out around them.
  6. Add the sunPaint full sun (6+ hours), part shade (3 to 6) and shade (under 3) onto the bed.

Then pick your plants: press + Add on any plant and it's placed for you, or use Plan it ▾ → Suggest a planting to let the planner choose. When you're happy, press Create PDF guide for a printable plan, plant list and care calendar.

Questions

Is my plan saved?

Yes, in this browser on this computer, automatically. To keep a copy, move it to another computer, or share it, press Save plan: it downloads a small file. Press Open plan to load it again.

Why can't I put a plant in some spots?

Each plant only goes where the light suits it. While you drag a plant, the spots that don't suit it turn gray. A plant may also need more room than is free.

How do I move, copy or remove plants?

Drag a plant to move it. Right-click any plant, object or empty spot for more choices. Shift-click a plant to select every plant of that kind. Undo and redo are just above the bed (Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y).

Does it work on a phone?

It's made for a computer, or an iPad or other tablet turned sideways. A phone can look around, but the bed is hard to work with on a small screen.

How do I start over?

Press Start over at the top of the planner. Save your plan first if you want to keep it.

Where the facts come from

Plant facts come from Michigan sources first, such as Michigan native plant nurseries and garden centers, then university extension services in nearby states. Every source and photo credit is listed on the Sources and photo credits page.
