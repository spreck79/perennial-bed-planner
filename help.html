Quick start

Work through the six steps at the top of the planner. Want it walked through? Press Guided tour. Just looking around? Load an example garden in step 1.

  1. Name your gardenOptional. It goes at the top of the planner and on your PDF guide.
  2. What kind of garden?Michigan natives, garden favorites or both, and the kinds of plants you like. Annuals (on by default) are flowers that live one summer: plant them after the last frost in late May and pull them out after frost in fall. The care calendar and your guide remind you. Shrubs (also on by default) are woody plants that come back bigger each year: each one says when and how to prune it to keep its size. Already have a shrub, tree or hedge in the bed? Add it in Add objects.
  3. Plants to watch out forLeave out plants that are toxic to pets or people, that deer eat, that need winter care, that bother ragweed allergies, or that can escape the garden.
  4. Your bedA rectangle, a round island, or draw your own outline. Set the size with − and +, and say which sides it's seen from.
  5. Add objectsPaths, stepping stones, rocks, a birdbath, shrubs or trees. Click one, then click the bed. Plants are laid out around them.
  6. How much sun?Pick the light most of the bed gets. If some spots are different, paint them: full sun (6+ hours), part shade (3 to 6) or shade (under 3).

Then pick your plants: press + Add on any plant and it's placed for you, or use Plan it ▾ → Suggest a planting to let the planner choose. When you're happy, press Create PDF guide.

Short lessons play the first time you draw a bed, add an object or paint the sun. A pointer shows each tool, then hands over to you. Skip closes one. To watch one again, press Show me how in that step. Start over plays them all again.

Find plants

The plant list only shows plants that fit your choices and the light in your bed. To narrow it down:

  • Search by name, Latin name or color.
  • Any light and Any height filter by where it grows and how tall it gets.
  • Flower color: click a dot to see only plants that bloom in that color. Click it again to show them all. Green means no flowers (ferns, sedges) or green flowers.
  • Group by: Show all (the usual) lists every plant in one list. Or sort them into folding groups: bloom season, plant type, native status, light, deer, or fall cleanup. Click a group's title to fold it.
  • All that fit shows every plant you could add. In your bed shows only the ones you've chosen.
  • Reset filters clears the search and filters and goes back to Show all.

Click a plant's photo to enlarge it. More info under the big photo opens that plant's own page.

The buttons above the bed:

  • + Add: a plant from a list, or an object to place.
  • Sun: paint sun and shade, change the light most of the bed gets, or clear what you painted.
  • Plan it: Suggest a planting picks plants for you. Re-arrange lays out the same plants again. Fill gaps tucks groundcover or companion plants into empty spots. Clear bed removes all plants.
  • View: show the plants as letters or photos, and turn the grid and sun colors on or off.

Lock in place (in a plant's menu) keeps a plant where you put it when the bed is re-arranged.

Read the plan

  • Each circle is the plant at its full grown size, so the plan doubles as your spacing guide. Grid squares are 1 foot.
  • The letters are a short code for the plant (BlSt for Blue Star). The key under the plan and the shopping list show which is which.
  • The small marks on a circle are the same icons as in the plant list: sun needs on top, the rest along the bottom.
  • The bed's colors show the light: yellow for full sun, tan for part shade, gray for shade.
  • A red dashed ring means the plant is too close to a neighbor or sits on an object. A yellow dotted ring means it's in light that doesn't suit it. Drag it somewhere better.
  • Full when grown (under the plan): 100% means every plant is at its recommended spacing, so the bed fills in once grown.
  • Seen from: the green edge is the side you look at the bed from. Short plants go there, taller ones behind.

Charts and shopping list

  • Garden Timeline: a bar for each plant showing the months it blooms, so you can spot gaps in the season. Striped bars are leaf plants that carry the bed between flowers. Switch between All plants and Chosen only.
  • Height and spacing: your chosen plants side by side from shortest to tallest, drawn to one scale. The bar under each is how far apart to set that kind.
  • Shopping list: what to buy for the bed as it's arranged, with how many of each.

Click a plant in any of them and it lights up on the bed plan and in the other charts, in the color of its last icon.

Plant pages

Every plant has its own page: photo, height and spacing, bloom months, every color it comes in, light, soil, hardiness, deer and toxicity, a year of care, the plants it goes well with, and where each fact came from. Open it with More info under a plant's enlarged photo. Print this plant makes a one-page PDF.

PDF guide

Create PDF guide builds a printable booklet for your bed:

  • A cover with your garden's name and a bloom calendar.
  • The shopping list.
  • The bed plan with a plant key, and a lay-it-out sheet to carry outside: set each pot on the center of its circle.
  • A card for each plant with its photo and facts.
  • A care calendar: what to do, month by month.
  • Photo credits.

It takes a few seconds to make. Then save it or print it from the viewer.

What the icons mean

Light it needs: full sun (6 or more hours of direct sun), part shade (3 to 6 hours) or shade (under 3). A blended icon means it takes more than one.
Michigan native: grows wild in Michigan. Good for local bees, butterflies and birds.
◆Garden favorite: not native, but sold at Michigan nurseries and garden centers. On a native, it means garden centers sell it too.
✿Daisy family: related to ragweed. Can bother people with ragweed allergies.
Deer eat it: rated "often" or "sometimes damaged" by Rutgers University.
❄Needs winter care: not hardy in zone 6B. It's dug up in fall and replanted in spring.
!Toxic plant: can make pets or people sick if eaten.
↔Can escape the garden: not native, and on a Michigan list of plants that spread into natural areas. The plant's page says how to keep it in bounds.

In the planner, point at (or tap) any icon to see what it means for that plant.

Questions

Is my plan saved?

Yes, in this browser on this computer, automatically. To keep a copy, move it to another computer, or share it, press Save plan: it downloads a small file. Press Open plan to load it again.

A plant I want isn't in the list. Where is it?

The list hides plants that don't fit your choices. Check three things: Reset filters in the plant list; the What kind of garden? and Plants to watch out for steps (a plant left out there is hidden); and the How much sun? step (a shade plant won't show for a bed that's all full sun). If it's still missing, the site may not have it yet: there are only so many plants so far.

Why can't I put a plant in some spots?

Each plant only goes where the light suits it. While you drag a plant, the spots that don't suit it turn gray. A plant may also need more room than is free.

I added a plant but it isn't on the bed.

There was no room for it in light that suits it, so it waits on the shopping list instead of going in the wrong spot. Make room (remove or move something, or make the bed bigger) and add it again.

How do I move, copy or remove plants?

Drag a plant to move it. Right-click it (on a tablet, press and hold) for Duplicate, Lock in place, Move to an open spot and Remove. See Work on the bed for the full list.

My garden is gone. What happened?

The plan is kept by your browser. It's lost if the browser's saved site data is cleared, if you used a private window, or if you're on a different browser or computer. Use Save plan now and then to keep a file you can open again anywhere.

The plants didn't load.

Refresh the page. If the list is still empty, hold Ctrl and press F5 (on a Mac, Cmd+Shift+R) to load a fresh copy.

Does it work on a phone or tablet?

It's made for a computer, or an iPad or other tablet turned sideways. On a tablet, press and hold takes the place of right-click, and two fingers zoom. A phone can look around, but the bed is hard to work with on a small screen.

Can I change my choices after I've planted?

Yes. Open any step again and change it. If a plant in the bed no longer fits, the planner asks before taking it out.

Does it work for my yard?

The plants and care dates are for Southern Michigan, zone 6B. Nearby zone 5 and 6 gardens will be close, but check a plant's hardiness zones on its page.

How do I start over?

Press Start over at the top of the planner. Save your plan first if you want to keep it. The lessons play again too.

Where the facts come from

Plant facts come from Michigan sources first, such as Michigan State University Extension, Michigan native plant nurseries and Michigan garden centers, then university extension services in nearby states. Deer ratings come from Rutgers University and toxicity from the ASPCA. Every plant's page lists its own sources, and every source and photo credit is on the Sources and photo credits page.

Feedback

This is a beta, so things may still change. Found a mistake or have an idea? Email feedback@spreck79.com. The link fills in the subject "Perennial Bed Planner feedback" for you.

Your email address is only used to reply to you. It's never added to a mailing list, shared, or used for anything else.

Privacy

No account, no ads and no cookies. Your garden is saved only in your own browser, and nothing about you is collected.

To see what people find useful, the site keeps simple counts: how many browsers visit each month, whether it's a computer, tablet or phone, the name of the site a visitor came from (such as Google), which plants get added and opened, the choices behind each PDF guide (like bed shape and main light), and whether the tour and lessons were watched or skipped. Your browser remembers which month it was last counted, so a visit is counted once a month. No name, email, internet address or location is sent, and the counts can't be traced back to anyone.

Switch it off and this browser is never counted. It stays off until you switch it back on.
